## Method

- Each answered question has hand-written SQL, and the number cells of its first row
  are the expected values.
- A question is correct when the status is the expected status and every expected
  value is the value of a claim that passed the verifier (exact for a whole number,
  0.01 for a decimal). An out-of-scope question must also call no tool.
- The expected values come from the local database, as `analyst_ro`. The local data
  is the same load as production.
- Seconds are the wall time of the scored question. Tokens and cost include the
  questions asked before it in the same conversation.

## Decision

The rule of the spec proposes `google_genai:gemini-3.5-flash-lite`.

Chosen: `google_genai:gemini-3.8-flash`. In the refresh of 2026-10-25 (24 questions, 2 runs each), Flash answered 24 of 24 in both runs and Flash-Lite 23 of 24. In both runs of q16 (the xG timeline of Tottenham v Arsenal, 2003-04), the claims of Flash-Lite passed the verifier but did not hold the xG totals of that match. A gap of one question is a tie, so the rule proposes gemini-3.5-flash-lite: about 6 times faster (4.7 s against 28.5 s median) and about 5 times cheaper ($0.008 against $0.037 a question). The current model stays. Flash-Lite missed q16 in both runs, and it failed the demo follow-up (q04, "Only the ones inside the box.") 2 times in 9 tries before this refresh: one tool error with no answer in a check of 5 conversations on 2026-10-14, and one run past the limit of 10 tool calls in the first evals. It passed q04 in both runs of this refresh. Flash has failed neither. Look at Flash-Lite again after a fix that makes both reliable on it. Production does not change.
